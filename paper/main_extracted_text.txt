An Intervention-Response Subspace Retains Nearly All
Readout-Relevant Prediction Error: A Case Study in Qwen3-0.6B
Rafał Wrona
Independent researcher
November 2026
Abstract
A rank-eight space fitted from native responses to an internal intervention, without readout
labels, retains almost all of the readout-relevant part of a vector predictor’s error. On fresh
Qwen3-0.6B layer-21 contexts the historical predictor lies in this space, so oracle repair inside it
inserts the projection of the true response: is/are readout MSE falls by 99.25%, and its readout
MSE is 0.47% of that of inserting no response (relative RMS error about 6.9%), although the
space holds only 28% of the vector-error energy. Post hoc, the space contains 29% of the native
readout-gradient energy, 37 times its random share, and the error outside it is nearly invisible
to the locally linear readout; rank-eight principal subspaces of natural activations contain about
4% and repair 7–8%. The intervention was selected earlier for its is/are effect. Response spaces
of three unscreened layer-19 neurons, even at matched response strength, repair only 0.2–3.6%,
like random spaces, so for the three tested alternatives the alignment does not transfer. Learning
the correction helps, but restricting it to the space does not: prior-centered ridge reduces the
historical predictor’s MSE by 97.21% with 384 labels, yet unrestricted ridge is better at 384
labels and, centered on the same prior, at 32 labels on a fourth fresh cohort, conditional on six
fits of which one drives much of the difference. Other layers, frames and checkpoints show both
benefit and harm of oracle repair.
1 Introduction
A model of an internal intervention is useful when its predicted vector change produces the right
downstream effect. This study asks whether a response space selected from native vector data
identifies a correctable component of prediction error, and whether that component can be learned
well enough to help on new contexts. We follow the same prediction through vector error, oracle
repair, learned correction, direct baselines and transfer.
The starting point is historical E041: on Qwen3-0.6B-Base, correcting error inside a rank-eight
response space reduced the is/are readout MSE by 98.85%, compared with 14.44% outside. The
original forecast favored outside repair and failed. The layer-21 regime is studied further because of
this unexpected favorable outcome, so fresh cohorts test replication of a selected observation, not a
randomlychosenlayer. Theintervenedlayer-19coordinatewasitselfidentifiedinearlierscreeningby
its effect on the is/are readout. Four subsequent 64-block TEST cohorts separate replication of the
oracle observation, learned correction, label-budget comparisons and a prior-matched comparison.
A later three-fit/readout study probes the stability of the small-budget result on a reused cohort,
and posthoc controls on the first cohort ask why the space works (Table 1).
The central finding is that almost all of the readout-relevant prediction error lies in a low-rank
space fitted to native intervention responses, while the larger error outside it is nearly invisible to the
1
readout. Post hoc, this space captures 29% of the readout-gradient energy against a random share
of 0.8%, while principal subspaces of natural activations capture about 4% and repair only 7–8%
of the readout error. Moving from oracle diagnosis to learned prediction changes the conclusion.
A space chosen without direct readout labels supports almost complete oracle correction on fresh
lexical pairs. A learned prior-centered correction substantially improves the historical predictor
on another fresh cohort. Full-vector direct ridge has lower mean loss at the matched additional
training budget. A correction advantage at 32 labels appears in one fixed training sequence; three
later FIT32 draws give mixed rankings, and on a fourth fresh cohort six new fits show no average
advantage of C over Direct and a disadvantage against full-vector ridge centered on the same prior.
Separate layer/frame tests include both benefit and harm. Together these results establish a useful
empirical structure whose predictive and comparative value depends on the fitting and evaluation
regime.

2 Relation to existing work
Engels et al. [4] decompose and partially repair sparse-autoencoder reconstruction error, measur-
ing downstream cross-entropy. Here the target is a native intervention delta and the outcome is
squared error in its predicted grammatical logit-margin effect. The empirical extension is the linked
sequence of response-space fitting, fresh oracle replication, rank/norm controls, learned correction
and resource-aware baseline comparison.
Sharma et al. [15] analyze low-rank subspaces of behavioral interventions and their relation
to decision directions; we instead ask which subspace of an intervention’s native response car-
ries the readout-relevant error of a vector predictor. Subspace activation patching, self-repair and
computational-graph methods provide established context [12, 14, 1, 5]. End-to-end dictionary
2
learning on downstream loss [2] and gradient-based attribution patching [16, 8] likewise separate
reconstruction or vector error from downstream-relevant change. The intervention edits a subject-
number coordinate; number units and causal agreement mechanisms were studied earlier [10, 6].
Deshpande [3] studies preservation of refitted ridge readouts under sparse compression; our spaces
instead derive from native vector responses and are tested by finite interventions through the native
suffix. Guo et al. [7] establish gradient-based intervention attribution and feature selection, directly
preceding the gradient forecasting used here. Luo et al. [11] show why intervention comparisons
need strength, surface and basis matching. Our random-space and norm controls address specified
parts of that comparison. Prior-centered ridge and kernel mean functions are standard regression
constructions [13]; correcting a historical predictor in this way is hypothesis transfer learning with
biased regularization [9], whose benefit with few target samples depends on how well the source hy-
pothesis fits the target task; negative transfer is possible. Tanyel and Ayvaz [17] show that subspaces
learned for a behavioral objective, such as distributed alignment search trained with cross-entropy,
can exceed the effect of whole-state patching mainly because of the objective, and that to first order
the effects of orthogonal parts add up to the whole. OurUwas fitted from vector responses with-
out readout labels before that work appeared, and our in-space/out-of-space split of the linearized
readout error uses the same first-order additivity; the intervention itself, however, was selected for
its readout effect. The contribution rests on the measurements and their controlled comparisons.

3.3 Sampling, inference and companion data
The independent sampling unit is the lexical block. The L21 analyses average 16 conditions within
each of 64 blocks; aligned studies average fits and conditions within each of 256 blocks. Reported
nominal 95% intervals use the saved 1,999-draw paired block bootstrap and condition on fixed
fitted objects. Original Gemma/Llama families retain their distinct saved correction rules. Each
prospective stage through the three-FIT32 study has its own independent scientific completion
receipt. The prior-matched study has an independent completion review (approved with changes; its
completion package remains open). The space controls are a posthoc analysis, not yet independently
reviewed, that fits principal subspaces and re-evaluates the native suffix and its gradient on cached
states. Otherwise the manuscript uses saved analyses without refitting or resampling.
The companion transcoder study shares Qwen L27 states and 4,096 unit-dose native/first-fit
ridge margins. Its ridge uses 128 FIT_MAP labels; Direct256 here uses a different pooled training
budget. The L21 cohorts and Gemma agreement tests have separate native acquisitions. Full reuse
mappings and model identities are in the supplement.

6 Limitations and interpretation
The evidence concerns selected synthetic contexts, native branches and grammatical two-token
margins. The intervened layer-19 coordinate was identified in earlier screening by its is/are effect, so
a response space fitted without readout labels is still conditioned on a readout-selected intervention;
response spaces of three unscreened interventions, including strength-matched versions, do not align
with this readout. The MLP-21 response carries a small part of the intervention’s readout effect
(root-mean-square about .15 logit, versus about .9 logit for the whole intervention), and the space
controls use one random draw and natural-activation principal spaces from 96 prompts. Fresh
lexical pairs test the historically selected L21 regime; they leave model/frame/layer generalization
conditional. The have/has extension changes a readout on reused prompts. Differences between

Oracle correction requires true TEST error. Learned C avoids that access, while still requiring
native upstream features and historical fitting information. Equal additional label budgets leave
those prior resources unequal. The prior-matched Direct_P comparison separates the prior from
the output-span restriction, conditional on six fits; its fit-level interval includes zero and one fit
drives much of the block-level difference. Kernel grids, fixed random draws and the three later
FIT32 cohorts bound the conclusions to the tested choices.
Intervals condition on fitted objects and sample lexical blocks. The newer nominal analyses,
older adjusted families and posthoc diagnostics have separate inferential scopes. In particular, two
negative per-fit C32 contrastscoexistwith a three-fit is/are intervalcrossing zero, and pointrankings
at budget256/384 leave the corresponding contrasts uncertain. Scientific completion approvals verify
the recorded execution and evidence; external replication remains open.
The resulting thesis is specific. In the historically selected L21 regime, a rank-eight space fitted
without readout labels to native responses of a readout-selected intervention retains almost all
of the readout-relevant part of fresh responses while discarding about half of their vector energy,
and it overlaps the native readout gradient far more than random or natural-activation principal
spaces. This is a measured posthoc association, not an established mechanism, and three unscreened
interventions of matched strength yield spaces with random-level repair. Other layers and models
remain untested. At 32 labels, learned use of this structure gained no demonstrated advantage from
the restriction to the space or from the historical prior over unrestricted ridge. Direct baselines, rank
controls and transfer tests define where that usefulness holds. The supplementary report provides
the complete historical record, forensic checks and operation receipts.
