1 Introduction
A vector predictor can approximate an intervention’s internal response yet mispredict its down-
stream effect. We ask whether a subspace fitted from native vector responses, without direct read-
out labels, identifies a repairable component of that error, and how far such a diagnosis transfers.
The question is empirical: reducing vector error need not reduce readout error, even for a linear
readout.
HistoricalexperimentE041foundanunusuallyconcentratedbenefitonQwen3-0.6B-Base: oracle
correction in a rank-eight response space reduced is/are readout MSE by 98.85%, versus 14.44%
in its complement. The original forecast had favored outside repair, so this was exploratory. We
now test the same frozen predictor, subspace, layer-21 target and upstream-neuron intervention on
64 fresh lexical blocks. Inside repair reduces MSE by 99.25%; outside by 8.38%. The result is no
longer supported only by the historical sample.
A separate three-fit study asks whether the benefit distinguishes FIT-derived spaces from simple
alternatives. At rank eight, oracle projection reduces mean loss by 88.13%, versus 1.18% for the fixed
same-rank random spaces and 5.30% for random corrections rescaled to the FIT correction norm.
These controls concern the new predictors, not a direct randomized comparison of the historical
predictor. All six ranks are retained; rank 32 improves further. Thus FIT-derived spaces identify
an oracle-correctable error component in this setting; the result does not identify a uniquely special
dimension eight.
The other studies establish limits. In Qwen’s separate layer-27 aligned study, the same three
fitted pairs help on T2 but harm on T5. Gemma-270M benefits on both frames; separate Gemma-1B
and Llama-1B cohorts establish T5 harm; Llama benefits on T2. A failed ranking prediction and
fitted-object substitutions constrain transfer. Finally, accurate oracle sign prediction does not imply
useful learned repair: a gate worsens the original predictor on Qwen T5, and a direct ridge map
trained on the same 256 available vector labels has lower loss on both frames. In contrast, a new
prospective L21 test now establishes useful learned correction relative to the historical predictor,
while full-vector direct prediction has lower mean loss.
The new L21 learned study reuses 384 existing FIT vector labels and freezes its models before
another 64-block TEST. A rank-eight residual correction reduces readout MSE by 97.21%, but
Direct384 has 23.97% lower mean loss than correction (equivalently, correction has 31.52% higher
loss). A direct refit restricted to the sameUspan also has slightly lower point loss. The contribu-
tion is this controlled chain: replicated oracle repairability, separate rank/norm controls, measured
learned utility against strong baselines, and context/fitted-object limits. Table S1 separates the co-
horts and inferential status. Four checkpoints appear, but the oracle and learned384 L21 tests use
one checkpoint and one readout; they are not four-model confirmation of low-dimensional repair.
The subsequent budget and new-FIT/readout studies sharpen that conclusion. A fixed 32-label C
predictor beats Direct32 on fresh is/are TEST, whereas Direct has lower mean loss at every larger
tested budget. Three disjoint new FIT32 cohorts then yield mixed rankings on the reused TEST:
the new have/has readout favors Direct on average, and the is/are interval crosses zero. The benefit
is observed in one fixed training sequence; the later three fits give mixed rankings and do not estab-
lish stable superiority across FIT draws or readouts. Historical priors consume additional resources
beyond the stated new-label budget.

2 Relation to existing work
The closest precedent is the decomposition and partial repair of sparse-autoencoder error by Engels
et al. [3]. That work studies natural-activation reconstruction error and downstream cross-entropy.
Here the target is anative intervention delta: a vector predictor approximates a donor-induced
branch response, and the outcome is squared error in its predicted is/are logit-margin effect. E041’s
space is selected from vector responses without direct readout labels; the fresh L21 replication
and separate rank/norm controls test that concrete concentration, while subsequent studies connect
repair effects to fitted-object substitutions and learned correction baselines. This difference specifies
the empirical question, not a claim of methodological superiority.
Subspace activation patching already has documented interpretability pitfalls [8]; self-repair
gives relevant context for compensating internal changes [10]. Counterfactual response subspaces
and geometry adaptation are also established topics [11, 6]. Gradient-aware sparsification and
native computational-graph methods precede this study [4, 1]. We therefore claim neither projection
repair nor Taylor/Jacobian forecasting as a new method. The contribution is the specific controlled
empirical chain summarized in Table S1, bounded to the reviewed literature rather than an assertion
of worldwide priority.

5 Repair is effective but conditional
5.1 Fresh replication of the low-dimensional benefit
Historical E041’s primary B5/F1 contrast gave 98.85% inside and 14.44% outside MSE reduction
on a 16-block MAIN cohort. Its original forecast favored outside repair, so the inside benefit
was exploratory. The fresh test preserves the selected fit and operator rather than retrospectively
changing that history. Table S3 now shows a 99.25% inside reduction on 64 new lexical blocks. The
paired inside-minus-B5 loss is−.0144172, with nominal 95% interval[−.0160698,−.0129072]. The
reduction is 99.07–99.30% across all four branch/dose subgroups. Outside improves by 8.38%, not
the historical 14.44%.
This confirms a specific empirical concentration: a space chosen from vector FIT responses
without direct readout supervision captures a component whose oracle correction almost eliminates
this readout error on fresh pairs. It does not show that eight coordinates explain the model, nor
that the two reduction percentages are additive contributions to a mechanism.

Nevertheless, direct prediction remains stronger overall. Learning residual coefficients under a
historical prior is therefore useful here without being a superior new learning method.

8 Discussion and conclusion
The fresh L21 studies identify a prediction-error component that is highly oracle-correctable in
the tested setting, then demonstrate learned usefulness on separate fresh data. The unchanged
historical rank-eight space supports a 99.25% oracle readout-MSE reduction; a learned coefficient
correction supports a 97.21% reduction relative to the historical predictor on another 64 blocks.
These are different cohorts and interventions on prediction error, not an equality between oracle
and learned performance. The space was fitted from native vector responses without direct readout
labels. Separate rank/norm controls favor FIT-derived spaces over the fixed random draws; ranks
16 and 32 improve further, so neither an intrinsic dimension eight nor optimality is established.
The baseline comparison is decisive for interpretation. Direct384 has lower mean loss than
learned correction despite using the same additional 384 vector labels, and a linear direct predictor
restricted toUalso has slightly lower point loss. A constant mean correction worsens the historical
predictor. The fresh budget TEST additionally identifies a conditional C32 advantage of 48.03%
over Direct32, but Direct has lower mean loss at 64–384 labels (the 256/384 contrast intervals
include zero) and C64 worsens relative to C32. Three new FIT32 cohorts on the reused TEST
12
favor Direct on average for have/has; the is/are mean interval includes zero. The C32 advantage
belongs to one fixed training sequence; two of the later is/are fits have negative point contrasts, but
the mixed three-fit result does not establish stable superiority. Thus the evidence supports scoped
learned utility and an observed conditional advantage. C is prior-centered kernel ridge, with an
exact linear prior interpretation under the conditions in Section 4.3; it is not a new or inherently
more expressive regression algorithm. Historical training information is not equalized by matching
the additional label count. Native prefix computation is still needed.
The L27/L17 and earlier tests establish limits rather than contradicting this L21 result. The
same three Qwen L27 fitted pairs help on T2 and harm on T5; Gemma-270M benefits on both, while
separate Gemma-1B and Llama-1B cohorts establish T5 harm; Llama benefits on T2. Their opera-
tors, layers and fitting recipes differ, preventing a causal attribution to layer alone. In L27, accurate
oracle sign forecasts coexist with a gate that worsens Qwen T5, and Direct256 has lower mean loss
on both frames. Repairability, learned utility and advantage over direct prediction therefore remain
distinct empirical questions.
Most conclusions concern one is/are margin in synthetic contexts; the posthoc have/has exten-
sion tests another grammatical margin on the same prompts, selected checkpoints/operators and
fixed fits. Fresh pairs do not undo historical selection of the favorable L21 regime. Nominal intervals
do not establish fit-population stability or familywise claims; fixed random controls do not sample
the entire distribution of possible spaces. The empirical contribution is the controlled sequence from
oracle-correctable error to measured learned utility and its limits, with negative baseline compar-
isons retained. Public release still requires complete authorship, evidence availability and campaign
disclosures.
